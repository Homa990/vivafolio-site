One sky, many eyes

Orion

The most famous shape in the night sky, and almost every people on Earth has seen something in it. Three bright stars in a perfect row are hard to ignore: a belt, a wrist, three brothers in a canoe, three kings, a spindle. Around them shine some of the brightest stars of all, one red and one blue-white. Orion lies on the sky’s equator, so it can be seen from every inhabited place on Earth, by every people who ever looked up on a winter night.

One sky, many eyes: its other names

  • Ōríōn (Ὠρίων) Ancient Greek: “the giant hunter”
  • Sah Ancient Egyptian: “the star-god, later the soul of Osiris”
  • MUL.SIPA.ZI.AN.NA Babylonian: “the true shepherd of the god Anu”
  • al-Jabbār (الجبّار) Arabic: “the giant”
  • Shekārchi (شکارچی) Persian: “the hunter”
  • Shēn (参) Chinese: ““three”: one of the 28 lunar mansions, named for the belt”
  • Tsuzumi-boshi (鼓星) Japanese: “the hand-drum stars, for the shape of a waisted drum”
  • Mṛgaśīrṣa (मृगशीर्ष) Sanskrit, India: “the deer’s head: the small triangle of stars at Orion’s head”
  • Nape Lakota: “the Hand: the belt is the wrist”
  • Djulpan Yolŋu, Arnhem Land: “the canoe with three brothers”
  • Friggerock Scandinavian folk names: “Frigg’s distaff: the belt as the goddess’s spinning tool”

Find it tonight

In November it rises in the east around 9 or 10 pm and is high in the south by 2 am; from December to March it is up all evening. Look for three bright stars in a short straight row. In the southern hemisphere Orion stands upside down, high in the north.

What the telescope says

  • Betelgeuse, the red star at his shoulder, is a supergiant: put in the Sun’s place, it would reach out beyond the orbit of Mars.
  • Rigel, at his foot, is a young blue-white star more than 100,000 times brighter than the Sun.
  • The misty spot in his sword is the Orion Nebula, about 1,300 light-years away: a cloud where new stars are being born right now.
  • The stars of Orion are not neighbours: they only line up from where we stand, at very different distances.
