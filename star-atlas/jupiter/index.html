One sky, many eyes

Jupiter

The king. Jupiter is huge, bright and steady, and it walks so slowly and evenly through the zodiac that people used it as a clock for years, not hours. Almost everywhere it was the planet of rulers and of good fortune.

Then they found it was slightly late, invented a shadow planet to fix it, and one of them may have seen something no one else would see for two thousand years.The astronomers of ancient China · 5 min read

One sky, many eyes: its other names

  • Phaethōn (Φαέθων) Early Greek: “the shining one”
  • Zeus, then Jupiter Greek, then Roman: “the king of the gods”
  • Marduk Babylonian: “the planet of Marduk, king of the gods of Babylon”
  • Hormozd, Barjīs, Moshtarī Persian: “the planet of good fortune, “the great luck” of the poems”
  • al-Mushtarī (المشتري) Arabic: “the planet of good fortune”
  • Suìxīng (歲星), now Mùxīng (木星) Chinese: “the year star; today, the wood star”
  • Bṛhaspati (बृहस्पति), Guru Sanskrit, India: “the teacher of the gods”

Find it tonight

After Venus, Jupiter is the brightest wanderer: a big, steady, creamy-white light, often the brightest point in the whole night sky. It moves slowly, about one zodiac sign a year, so it stays in your sky for months. Hold a pair of binoculars very still and you may see up to four tiny stars in a line beside it: Galileo’s moons.

What the telescope says

  • The largest planet: more than 1,300 Earths could fit inside it.
  • It spins faster than any other planet: a day on Jupiter lasts under ten hours.
  • It goes around the Sun in almost twelve years.
  • The Great Red Spot is a storm wider than Earth that has been raging for centuries.
  • It has more than a hundred known moons, and astronomers keep finding more.
