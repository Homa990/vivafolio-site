One sky, many eyes

The Pleiades

A tiny, shining knot of stars, so small you could hide it behind your fingertip held at arm’s length. Look straight at it and it seems to blur; look a little to one side and more stars appear. Almost every people on Earth has noticed it, counted its stars, and told a story about it. Strangely often the story is about seven sisters, and someone chasing them. Farmers planted by it, sailors steered by it, and whole nations still begin their new year when it returns.

One sky, many eyes: its other names

  • Pleiades (Πλειάδες) Ancient Greek: “the daughters of Pleione; the word also sounds like “doves””
  • Parvin (پروین) and Sorayya (ثریا) Persian: “two beloved names in Persian poetry; Sorayya comes from the Arabic”
  • al-Thurayyā (الثريا) Arabic: “the little abundant ones”
  • Subaru (昴) Japanese: “gathered together”
  • Mǎo (昴) Chinese: “one of the 28 lunar mansions, the “Hairy Head””
  • Kṛttikā (कृत्तिका) Sanskrit, India: “the six nurses who raised the war god Kārttikeya”
  • Matariki Māori: “the eyes of the god Tāwhirimātea”
  • Makaliʻi Hawaiian: “its rising at sunset opens the Makahiki season of peace and harvest”
  • Qullqa Quechua, the Andes: “the storehouse, the granary”
  • Tianquiztli Nahuatl, the Aztecs: “the marketplace”
  • Kimah (כימה) Hebrew, the Book of Job: ““Can you bind the chains of the Pleiades?””
  • The Seven Sisters Many peoples, from Greece to the Western Desert of Australia: “seven women, very often chased”

Find it tonight

From October to March. In November it rises in the east as night falls and is high in the south (in the north, from the southern hemisphere) around midnight. Follow Orion’s belt up and to the right, past the orange star Aldebaran, to a small silver mist of stars.

What the telescope says

  • A real family of stars, born together, about 440 light-years away.
  • Young for stars: about 100 million years old. The Sun is more than 4.5 billion.
  • Most eyes see six or seven stars. A telescope shows more than a thousand.
  • In photographs they sit in a blue haze: a cloud of dust the cluster happens to be drifting through.
